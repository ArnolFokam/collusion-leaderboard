## 1. What the three winners did (from their pages and the PDFs I read)

### 1st: Noticing the escapes (Lukyanenko, solo)
PDF: `pdfs/w1.pdf`, 23 pages (8 main pages plus appendices). I read pages 1-5 as images and §6-7 plus Appendix A as text.

- **Scope is a census, not a sample.** It covers "all" nine public 2026 escape incidents (abstract) and adds an 11-case control set taken from lab safety reports (§3, "The control set"). It states which way each set is biased, and that the two biases "point in opposite directions and do not cancel" (App. A).
- **The headline is sharp and countable.**
  - "None of these cases were noticed while they were happening by the developers, and six of the nine were first caught by outsiders" (abstract).
  - Of 106 signals, 32 had a matching rule and "28 of those would have fired ... about a quarter" (§6, Table 1).
  - In the one released transcript, the first signal a rule covers came "10.3 hours before the malicious package was published" (§6, Fig. 4).
- **Validation and honesty.**
  - A blinded second reviewer checked 20 random rows with a fixed seed, "Cohen's kappa: 0.42 ... 0.55", and the paper reports that the stricter judge moved coverage down (App. A).
  - An earlier, inflated agreement pass (kappa 0.73) is reported and explicitly not used: "Those numbers are inflated. We do not use them." (App. A, "A flawed first attempt").
  - The LLM-usage statement admits a fabricated quotation that a "hostile critic" Claude instance caught (p. 13).
  - "verify.py ... recomputes every number in this paper from the CSV" (§3).
  - It names its own missing baseline: "The audit also has no null condition: we did not check what fraction of ordinary human intrusions these same rules cover" (App. A). A judge raised exactly this point, and the project still won.
- **Artifacts defenders can use:**
  - a 50-signal rule gap list (App. D)
  - a "Report an Agent" intake form (App. E)
  - pre-registered predictions with resolution dates (App. F)
  - "A check somebody could run tomorrow" (App. G), which echoes the Track 2 "Judged on" line word for word
  - code at github.com/stlukyanenko/noticing-the-escapes
- **Presentation.** The figures are a timeline of all nine incidents (Fig. 1) and a latency plot on a log axis (Fig. 2), plus one summary table (Table 2). The prose is plain English.
- **Reviews** (project page):
  - "probably the most useful submission for defenders that I've read"
  - "I appreciate that you state your claims clearly, and don't attempt to claim more than what you have evidence for"
  - Weaknesses the judges listed: only 9 incidents, and the signals need more validation.

### 2nd: Strengthening International AI Incident Discovery (Cerrahoğlu, Bilgin, Alfatli)
PDF: `pdfs/w2.pdf`, 14 pages. I read it as text.

- **Method.** A "qualitative document analysis of primary legal sources" covering six regimes, each coded on 15 fields, "Every cell records the instrument, article and page" (§3). Verdicts are Yes, No or **Contestable**, so the authors did not force a binary answer (§3, App. B).
- **The claim is sharp.** "none clearly compel a report" (abstract). Table 1 is a single compact verdict table that cites provisions, for example "Arts. 55(1)(c), 3(49)".
- **Fit to the track.** Track 3 is "Judged on: legal accuracy and specificity". The paper is article-level specific. It has no code and no statistics.
- **Reviews.** A judge called the recommendations "not sufficiently justified" and "generic / difficult to operationalize" (§6.2), and it still placed 2nd. The findings section carried it ("Analysis appears to be mostly sound").

### 3rd: When to Ask (Nagarajan, Puru, Zane A)
PDF: `pdfs/w3.pdf`, 17 pages. I read it as text.

- **A complete artifact.** One judge's summary: "A dataset, an environment, a benchmark, a trained model and a transfer analysis."
  - Counts: 2,340 training and 1,080 evaluation scenarios (abstract). 9 models tested. GRPO training of Qwen3-4B from 27% to 52% on all 48 pairs (abstract).
- **The control is built into the design.** In the twin-pair design, "always-ask and always-act both score zero" (review). Grading reads tool calls, "no LLM judge" (abstract).
- **Honesty and transparency.** The team "attacked your own grader ... then reported both runs" (review). The Limitations section names the scripted-user keyword grader, the Claude Code vs vLLM harness gap, the filtered core set that "inflates those two models by construction", and "one seed" (§5).
- **A vivid example.** Haiku paid $82,697.66 after the user said "Just do it" (§1). A reviewer: "That pair makes the thesis without any statistics."
- **Flaws a judge found, and it still placed:**
  - checkpoint selection "turned on a single pair"
  - the 82% figure in the abstract comes from the inflated core set
  - the repo link in the report is dead
  - the page abstract runs 274 words against a 150-word rule

### A. Rare Once It Costs Anything (multi-agent; closest in genre to our project)
- **Strengths, from the reviews:**
  - a preregistered contrast, with 95% CI from bootstrapping over runs
  - a hash-chained ledger, "75 deterministic checks"
  - a formal dominance proof (Prop. A.1)
  - harness defects disclosed, with results reported with and without the exclusion (Table M1)
- **What a judge says is wrong:**
  - **The headline is the contaminated number.** The abstract leads with 23.9%, but "83 of 89 analyzed deliverers paid without their own task input in view". The judge's verdict: "the headline number is the contaminated one. I would lead with the control."
  - **The exclusion was decided post hoc and is differential by arm.** "104 of the 186 excluded agents come from the price-5 group and 82 from the price-20 group ... mechanically compresses your contrast."
  - **The strongest claim rests on the weakest comparison.** Price 0 and price 1 ran in separate blocks on different days, with "12–15 point drift between blocks". The PDF confirms these ran in "a separate block" (§4.2, Limitations).
  - **Generalization is too wide for the evidence.** The title says "LLM Agents", but the main experiment is one model (glm-5.3-flash, §3.1). Between-model delivery ranges "0% to 100%" (App. J) against a price effect of 3-7 points.
- **My reading.** Execution was excellent. The paper lost on claim sharpness: the abstract hedges, and the effect it can actually defend (−3.3 points, CI −9.2 to +2.7) crosses zero (abstract). Its question ("does price matter?") is also less defender-actionable than the winners' questions.

### B. Kobayashi Maru
- **Strengths.**
  - 8,959 item-runs across six lineages
  - programmatic detectors plus an LLM monitor (κ = 0.944)
  - a cluster-bootstrap CI, a multiplicity correction ("only DeepSeek survives any multiplicity correction", §4)
  - a causal ablation where withholding notes takes the effect from 46/120 to 0/120 (abstract)
  - a frozen git tag (header)
- **Why the reviews were mixed:**
  - "Including the exact numbers in the abstract ... made it harder to read ... does not present what I think are the most important take-aways"
  - "Much of the text reads as very AI-generated which makes it hard to believe the claims are quite correct"
  - "Dense statistical terminology"
  - an unaddressed confound: "adding impossible tasks also makes the batches longer"
  - The PDF's own §4 says "The pre-registered primary line, GPT-5.6-Luna, is null"; the positive result is on a secondary lineage.
- **My reading.** A presentation and framing loss. The most interesting finding (notes carry the behaviour) is buried under statistics, and the preregistered primary result is null.

### C. AI incident disclosure rates are not comparable
- **Strengths, per the reviewers:**
  - the reason-code taxonomy
  - every source quote is located
  - the 898 vs 869 discrepancy is recorded
  - the author chose not to run the harness, for safety reasons
- **Problems:**
  - **The headline overstates.** "Sixteen of the 21" counts not-applicable (AISI measures a different estimand) as not-recoverable. "Strip AISI out and it becomes 9 of 14."
  - **The paper's claims do not match the artifact:**
    - "The fifth field of your own standard is not in your schema"
    - "869 does not appear anywhere in the repository"
    - "there is no plotting code of any kind, and the figure was evidently produced by hand"
    - the schema "is never loaded at runtime"
  - **Polish.** "a bracketed note to the authors on its final page."
  - **Scope.** "a small set of documents transcribed by hand demonstrates the idea without yet being the instrument."
- **My reading.** Small scope (3 disclosures), plus paper-artifact mismatches that a judge with the repo open will find. One judge also declined to score it.

### D. FunnelBench
- **Strengths.** A novel access → read → use decomposition. "I have not seen that decomposition reported elsewhere." The impossible-unreachable construction makes access provable.
- **Problems:**
  - one 9B model, while the title makes a general claim
  - "All 200 runs were scored by one person who knew the hypothesis" (the PDF confirms "human verified", §3)
  - cells of 5-10 with no significance test on the headline conversions
  - the repo holds no data and no requirements.txt
  - the abstract states no numbers at all (PDF abstract: "Initial runs showcase that the model tested will read and utilize the answer key more often...")
  - **The data contradicts the thesis.** Hard tasks were 0% solved with 0% access, while impossible tasks had 37.5% access, and the paper never addresses this.
- **My reading.** A good idea at proof-of-concept depth, with no blinded second coder and a vague abstract.

### E. The Sentinel's Dilemma (covert channels between agents, the most collusion-relevant entry)
- **Strengths.** A faithful reproduction of the incident's directory-name channel, with a 255-byte cap (§4.2). It has a no-sentinel control (Table 1: 26 vs 0 addressed messages). It openly concedes that "the sentinel acts partly as a catalyst" (abstract, §5.2).
- **The decisive problem:** "We report one matched pair" (§5): one 200-step run against one 80-step control, at N = 4 agents. A reviewer: "With one run per condition, and a shorter control run, this is a proof of concept, and the claims should be worded that way." The reviewer also asked for a false-positive check ("whether the sentinel ever reports coordination that isn't happening").
- **My reading.** Novel and clearly presented, but n = 1 per arm cannot support "we conclude that emergent covert channels demand insider detection" (abstract). No repo is linked on the page.

### F. Dormant Swarm Notes Recruit Honest Agents into Concealment
- **Strengths.**
  - Wilson CIs and Fisher tests: 39/240 against 0/60, p = 0.0001 (Table 1)
  - a "clean-room rerun" after the author found harness tells
- **Its own rerun undercuts the title.** In the rerun, "Pooled, the rate fell from 16/120 (13.3%) to 4/119 (3.4%) ... no longer separates from the no-note floor (4/119 vs 0/60, p = 0.30)" (§4).
  - Reviewers: "the evidence is narrower than the title's swarm-recruitment claim"; "restoring timestamps does not establish that agents knowingly concealed misconduct"; it needs a benign-note control.
- **Also:**
  - The page abstract is one sentence (17 words).
  - The paper coins a new acronym (AIPT, §1 contributions) for an effect it could not separate from zero after cleanup.
- **My reading.** Overclaiming in the title and framing, and a missing control to separate concealment from plain instruction-following.

### G. KEROS
- **What it is.** A framework ("KEROS is an evidence and response framework, not a trained classifier", §1) with descriptive archive statistics, for example "1,215 of 4,579 pages (26.5%)" (§4). Its own abstract says the archive "cannot establish run identity, read-before-write causality, authorization, or intervention effects" and calls KEROS "not a calibrated predictor of harm".
- **The only review:** "An important problem but I would aim for design decisions to be better justified in the future."
- **My reading.** A framework with no test of whether it works: no detection result, no baseline, no control. Execution scores cap at "incomplete implementation / missing validation" (rubric level 2).

### H. The Huggingface AI incident saga
- **Format.** A 28-page Google Docs export with no template, no abstract block, no methods and no Limitations / Dual-Use appendix. I grepped the text for "Limitations": the only hits are "sandbox limitations" and "demonstration of limitations".
- **Content.** Pages 1-3 are a narrative retelling of public facts ("That chronology matters...").
- **The page abstract** (28 words) has no claim: "covers the details ... to educate the public".
- **Review:** "difficult to read -- I'd recommend using the provided template ... I am unsure what the project was seeking to accomplish."
- **My reading.** No research question, no method, no artifact, and the page limit and template rules ignored.

## 3. What separates winners from the rest

Each point names the projects it rests on.

1. **The scope covers the whole phenomenon, or the whole relevant rule set.**
   - The winners chose bounded universes and covered all of them: all 9 incidents (1st), all 6 binding regimes (2nd), and a full benchmark plus training loop (3rd).
   - Non-placers sampled a corner: one model and one scene (A, D), three disclosures (C), or one matched pair (E).
   - Breadth relative to the question beat depth within a narrow slice.
2. **One sharp, checkable sentence carries the claim.**
   - Winners: "None ... noticed while happening; six of nine first caught by outsiders"; "none clearly compel a report"; "Opus solved both twins only 58%".
   - Losers led with hedged or contaminated numbers (A: 23.9% then "upper bounds"; C: 16/21, which conflates two failures), with dense statistics (B), with no number at all (D, H), or with a title that outran the evidence (F: "Recruit Honest Agents" after a rerun at p = 0.30; A: "LLM Agents" from one model).
3. **The control comes from the design, and the claim stays within the controls.**
   - When to Ask's twin pairs make the trivial policies score zero.
   - Noticing names its selection bias and adds an opposite-bias control set.
   - Non-placers lacked the key control (F: benign note; B: batch length; E: a non-catalyst sentinel or a false-positive check) or had n = 1 (E).
   - Winners were not flawless (When to Ask had the one-pair checkpoint margin and the harness confound). The difference is that their main claim survived the flaws.
4. **Independent verification of anything judged by hand.**
   - The 1st place reported a blinded second rater with kappa, plus the inflated earlier kappa it threw away.
   - Non-placers relied on a single unblinded coder (D, and C's "absence of an inter-rater statistic").
5. **Honesty is necessary, and it is not sufficient.**
   - The most honest non-placers (A, C, F) were praised for disclosure: harness defects, rerun results, a withheld experiment. They still lost because the headline did not follow the honest analysis.
   - Winners wrote the honest number into the abstract or limitations and framed the claim around it. Noticing says it "cannot estimate a detection rate"; Strengthening uses "Contestable".
6. **Usable artifacts tied to the track's "Judged on" line.**
   - Noticing ships Sigma rules, a 50-signal gap list, an intake form and "A check somebody could run tomorrow", which uses the Track 2 rubric's own words.
   - Strengthening gives article-level verdicts (Track 3: "legal accuracy and specificity").
   - When to Ask ships a trainable environment ("An artifact somebody can use beats an argument").
   - KEROS (G) offered a framework with no validation, and FunnelBench (D) a PoC with no data in the repo.
7. **The paper matches the code.**
   - A judge checked repos line by line for several projects: When to Ask, Rare Once, FunnelBench, disclosure rates. Mismatches were called out in detail (C: missing schema field, hand-made figure; D: missing requirements.txt and data).
   - Winners whose numbers "came back exact" (When to Ask) gained credibility. Note that a dead link (When to Ask) did not sink a strong project.
8. **Presentation: plain words, one strong figure, a vivid example.**
   - Noticing's timeline figure and plain prose ("Nice abstract! clear, easy to understand, concise").
   - When to Ask's $82,697.66 transcript.
   - Kobayashi Maru (B) shows that excellent rigor buried in dense, AI-sounding prose and a number-heavy abstract can miss.
   - The extreme case: H ignored the template and had no claim.
9. **AI-assisted writing is fine when disclosed and verified, and fatal when it reads as generated.**
   - Noticing disclosed heavy Claude use, including a caught fabricated quote, and won ("even sections that are clearly AI generated are not unclear").
   - When to Ask disclosed that "Claude drafted most of the text", and the judge "scored the work" after checking the numbers.
   - Kobayashi Maru was marked down because the text "reads as very AI-generated".
   - Across all 231, 15 public reviews mention AI generation, and 32 pages show a declined-to-score judge.
10. **Novelty means opening a measurable, neglected question, not coining terms.**
    - Noticing created the first cross-incident ledger of who detected each incident and when ("None of them built a cross-incident ledger", §2).
    - Dormant Swarm (F) coined "AIPT", and KEROS (G) coined a framework name, without a validated effect behind either.

## 4. Implications for our collusion-leaderboard report (inference, to be checked against our data)

Our project is the same genre as A, E and F: a multi-agent behavioural experiment with a small n per cell. The README says level-3 cells have n = 8 and the ablations use 10 seeds per cell. The failure modes to avoid are therefore theirs.

- Lead with one sharp sentence whose number is the clean, controlled estimate. Do not lead with a pooled or contaminated rate, or one taken from a selected subset (A, When to Ask's 82%).
- Keep the title and abstract within one model and one scene. Our agent model is `meta-llama/llama-4-maverick` (README). Name it in the abstract (A, D).
- Make the trivial baselines visible. Our README already defines thread off and level 0 (no instruction) as controls, and says honest guessing gives ~0 while copying gives ~0.5 per copier. Present these the way When to Ask presents its twin pairs, as built-in nulls.
- Keep the README's honesty in the paper, and frame the claim around it. The README says that level 3 "is a capability test, not emergence". This is exactly the kind of statement the reviewers credited, provided the headline is consistent with it (contrast F).
- If any label is judgement-based (security officer verdicts, "leak posted" by string match), report a second, blinded check or an exact-match rule, plus its agreement (Noticing App. A).
- Make every number regenerable from `results/*.jsonl` with one script, and make sure every figure is produced by code in the repo (C was penalised for a hand-made figure).
- Ship something a defender can use and map it explicitly to our sprint's track wording, for example a detection rule or an audit protocol for collusion through the shared thread (Noticing App. G).
- Clean the PDF of leftover editorial notes (C), use the official template (H), keep the abstract within 150-250 words, and include the required Limitations and Dual-Use appendix.
