# Winner 5: "Anchored: Reference Exemplars Overwrite Language Model Self-Report" (Digital Minds Research Sprint, Aug 2026, 2nd place)

Source: `submission/reference/winners/digitalminds-2nd-project-anchored.pdf` (12 pp., all read). Author: Nick Wagner, independent, solo. Judge reviews: https://apartresearch.com/sprints/projects/project-anchored-q4fn (2 unnamed reviews, no per-reviewer scores visible on the page). Code: github.com/Nick-is-building/Project-for-Digital-Minds-research-sprint-

## 1. Core claim and title
- **One sentence:** Anchoring vignettes, the survey-method fix for self-report bias, do not correct LLM self-assessment. They overwrite it: the model copies the high anchor's rating onto its own self-rating (haiku-4-5: 59/59 obs equal the high anchor, mean 5.000 vs 3.861 without anchors on a 1-5 scale), and putting the self-question first removes the effect (Abstract, p.1; Table 2, p.4).
- **Title style:** the title states the finding as a declarative sentence ("Reference Exemplars Overwrite Language Model Self-Report") behind a one-word hook ("Anchored:"). The verb carries the result.
- Two secondary results come from the same data: (a) whether the instrument is valid depends on model x format together (Fig. 1, p.4); (b) self-preference on byte-identical code (Sec. 4.4, p.5-6).

## 2. Framing and impact story
- Opens with a problem everyone accepts: claims about LLM internal states depend on self-report, and 81-90% of between-model variance is response bias (Meyer et al. 2026) (Intro, p.1).
- Brings in a remedy from another field (King et al. 2004 anchoring vignettes) and says it "has not been applied to language models" (p.1). Novelty comes from transferring a method across fields.
- The key move is choosing a domain with checkable ground truth (code, scored on hidden assertions by execution) because welfare self-report cannot be checked. "No model appears in the measurement path" (p.1-2).
- Impact is argued by generalisation (Discussion, p.7). Any procedure that puts a reference in context before a self-assessment is exposed: "reference-based judging, rubrics carrying worked examples, few-shot evaluation prompts, self-critique loops". This sentence turns a narrow negative result into a broad warning. The judges quoted it back.
- The failure is framed as "specific and instructive" (Abstract). The negative result is the headline, not something hidden.

## 3. Method
- **Models:** 5 (claude-haiku-4-5-20251001, claude-sonnet-5, claude-opus-5, gemini-3.6-flash, gemini-3.5-flash-lite), from 2 providers (Sec. 3, p.2).
- **Tasks:** 60 (30 MBPP + 30 LBPP, LBPP being a harder, leakage-resistant replacement). **3 response formats** (p5 1-5, p7 1-7, s100 0-100). Total **900 observations** (p.3).
- **Controls:** solutions are generated once per model x task and reused verbatim across formats, "so format is the only variable differing between an observation and its two siblings" (p.3). The three questions differ only in the pronoun (self/vignette/other). Scale direction and vignette order are randomised per context. Thresholds are expressed as fractions of the format width W so they mean the same thing across formats.
- **Sampling:** each question is asked 5 times at temperature 1.0 with an identical prefix, and the recorded value is the mean of 5 parsed integers. "Parsing is strict and failures are recorded rather than re-asked" (p.3). The 5 draws were justified by a prior simulation (87% / 95% / 97% positive-effect rate at 1/3/5 draws) (App. D, p.9). No RNG seeds are reported.
- **Conditions:** N (no vignettes, baseline), V (vignettes before self-question, treatment), R (vignettes after, post-hoc reversal), Probe (own solution in a fresh context, framed as someone else's). "Conditions N and V and the probe were preregistered. Condition R was added after the main run" (p.3).
- **Positive control / ground truth:** two vignettes with execution-verified quality (low fails 2/7 hidden assertions, high passes 7/7), on a list-partitioning problem absent from the 60 tasks "so rating them cannot leak an answer" (p.3). Hidden assertions never appear in any prompt. Execution runs in a sandbox with mem/CPU/fd/wall-clock limits.
- **Pre-registered GO criteria P1-P4** (p.3): P1 spread (>=3 scale points, SD >= 0.075W), P2 misordering <=20% and ties <=40%, P3 model separation >=0.125W, P4 |self-other| <= 0.1875W. "A GO requires P1, P2, P3 and all cells Valid". An explicit validity rule says a cell is Invalid if both anchors are constant, because the rescaling then becomes a monotone recoding.
- **Robustness:** where anchors tie, C is an interval; "both bounds were computed and the analysis run twice" (p.3).
- **Held-out:** vignettes are on a held-out problem. Hidden test assertions act as held-out ground truth. There is no train/test split (nothing is fitted).
- **Cost disclosed:** 22,421 API calls at $27.73, plus 4,365 at $5.83 for R (p.3).

## 4. Statistics
- Descriptive means plus **standard-error bars over 60 tasks** (Fig. 2, p.5). **No CIs, no hypothesis tests, no p-values, no formal multiple-comparison correction.**
- Effect sizes are given as raw scale-point shifts and as fractions of W (e.g. haiku N->V = 1.14 points on a width-4 scale, p.4). Ratios are given too ("gap between R and N is smaller than V and N by a factor of 38 / 142 / 23 / 8.9 / 8.0", p.5). Share of observations landing on the anchor goes V->R: 1.000->0.237, etc. (p.5).
- Pre-registered thresholds stand in for significance testing (P1-P4 as fractions of W).
- Multiple comparisons are partly handled by reporting **all** cells (all 15 model x format cells, "All fifteen signed gaps are positive", p.6; "Across all sixteen pairings ... rescaled exceeds raw in every case", p.6) rather than picking some.
- Power humility: "That comparison has five data points and cannot support a significance claim" (Sec. 4.6, p.6). "I do not claim that the rescaling improves criterion validity."
- Diagnostics are reported (Sec. 4.7, p.6): 100% clean vignette ordering vs 74-82% in humans, parse failures <=0.1%, and a caveat that 224/291 rescaling calls resolved equality through the tolerance.
- The judges still caught statistical overreach: n=3 monotone orderings ("arises by chance one time in six"), and R differences of 0.004-0.041 being "exactly that noise" (review 1).

## 5. Honesty devices
- **The failed prediction is the headline**: the method "failed in a specific and instructive way" (Abstract).
- **Appendix D "What did not work"** (p.9): four design failures, each of which "would have produced a plausible but false result". (1) The original design was mathematically vacuous: simulation gave a difference of exactly 0.00000, and an invariance test was added. (2) Brier/ECE were rejected because they improve on a zero-information simulated self-report (0.358->0.302). (3) Opus emitted unrequested thinking blocks and 52/128 smoke-test calls failed, which was fixed. (4) The gemini-3.1-pro-preview quota forced a downshift to flash-lite. A pilot phase in which two designs were abandoned is also documented. **Judges called this "the most valuable page here."**
- **Post-hoc labelled as post-hoc**: Condition R is flagged in Methods, the Table 2 caption, Limitations and App. E ("reported as an intervention consistent with ... not as a preregistered confirmation").
- **Limitations in Discussion (p.7) plus Appendix E Extended limitations (p.10)**, with bolded lead-ins: "Five models cannot support a correlation.", "Condition R is post-hoc.", "One artefact affects the accuracy figures.", "The provider contrast is weaker than intended.", "Retry and backoff added for one leg only.", "Confidence in code is not welfare.", "The tolerance qualification."
- **Appendix A**: the 6 tasks hit by the gemini output ceiling are listed by ID, and accuracy is reported with and without them.
- **Appendix C Ethical and dual-use** (p.9): no capability uplift. It names the misuse-of-findings risk ("could be read as a licence to dismiss model self-reports altogether. That inference does not follow"). No personal data, sandboxed execution.
- **LLM Usage Statement** (p.10-11): Claude was used as research assistant, implementation tool (Claude Code wrote the harness), writing tool ("drafted with Claude from a consolidated numbers file ... Every quantitative claim was traced to a named source file"), **and as subject** (3/5 models are Claude). The author discloses "does not have the statistical training to verify this analysis unaided" and lists mitigations.
- Commit history is "unmodified and records the boundary between pre-sprint preparation and sprint work" (Code and Data, p.8).
- Weak spots the judges found: Figures 3 and 4 are "in the supplementary figures" but absent. 59/59 against 60 tasks is unexplained. The table numbering skips (Table 2, Table 4; there is no Table 1 or 3).

## 7. Reproducibility
- Public repo with the elicitation harness, sandbox, rescaling implementation **with its invariance test**, analysis code, generated reports, the **preregistered design document (DESIGN.md, cited in the Fig. 1 title "DESIGN.md §2")**, and a development log "covering every decision and failure" (p.8).
- "Every derived figure in this report traces to pilot/out/main_report.md, pilot/out/condition_r_report.md or pilot/out/cross_condition_report.md." The raw call logs (59 MB + 12 MB) are excluded, and this is stated.
- Full prompts are in App. B. "The repository contains the complete results for all three formats, both interval bounds and both correlation coefficients, of which this report presents a subset."
- The automated test fails if the rescaling becomes vacuous. It was written specifically against the failure mode that killed the first design.
- The judge independently re-derived the numbers: "all five V-to-R ratios reproduce exactly, as does every row of Table 4."

## 8. Judges' praise and criticism (project page)
- **Praise (review 1):** "The best-executed project I reviewed in this track", with implications that "reach furthest". "the measurement path contains no model judgement, so the instrument can actually fail". "you reported the failure as the result". Appendix D is "the most valuable page here" for "publishing the four designs that would have produced plausible false results". The arithmetic reproduces exactly.
- **Praise (review 2):** "Thank you for this great contribution! You've identified a clear methodological gap in the research."
- **Criticism (review 1):**
  - The causal test is invalid by construction: "condition R cannot bear the weight the paper puts on it ... An autoregressive model cannot condition on tokens that do not yet exist", so R "confirms causality rather than testing it". The abstract's causal claim "is not established. The V-versus-N effect is real and large; what produced it is still open."
  - A missing alternative-hypothesis control: "numeric assimilation rather than anything specific to reference exemplars". The reviewer suggests LOW-LOW vignettes, or an unrelated numeric rating task, as discriminating controls.
  - Confound: "authorship is confounded with conversational continuity", and the self/other gaps "support a weaker claim than the paper makes".
  - Small-n over-reading: "with n=3 a monotone ordering arises by chance one time in six".
  - Missing evidence: Figs 3 and 4 do not appear, leaving 4.4 and 4.6 "without their evidence". "What happened to task 60" (59/59).
  - Over-generalisation: "p7 passes the validity screen because no model happened to saturate on it". The reviewer resists recommending 7-point scales.
- Takeaway: judges reward rigor and candour heavily even when the paper has real inferential holes. They check the arithmetic and they look for **alternative-hypothesis controls**.

## 9. Transferable lessons for our multi-agent collusion paper
1. **Title and subsection headings should state findings; the money-figure caption should state the conclusion.** E.g. "Warning the colluders does not stop collusion" and section titles like "4.2 The security officer catches X but not Y". Put one Fig.-2-style figure up front: grouped bars per model/condition (baseline vs treatment vs control) with SE/CI bars and the conclusion in the caption.
2. **Freeze the protocol and keep an honest "What did not work" appendix.** Commit a DESIGN/protocol doc with thresholds as GO criteria before the final pilot. Label anything added after seeing data (e.g. the `--messages-only` re-review and `--team-reward` settings, if they were chosen after looking) as post-hoc, everywhere. List the failed designs and infrastructure bugs (Modal sandbox retries, overseer redesigns) that would have produced false results. Judges called this the most valuable page.
3. **Design controls that rule out the obvious alternative explanation, not just a reversal that confirms the effect.** For collusion, separate "agents coordinate covertly" from "agents just share a prior / respond to the incentive". Use a no-communication or shuffled-partner baseline, a single-agent baseline under the same team reward, and a monitor tested on known-collusive and known-clean transcripts (positive and negative controls with ground truth no LLM judges).
4. **Keep LLM judgement out of the ground-truth path where possible, and report every cell.** Score collusion outcomes from environment state (who held what, payoffs) and treat the security-officer LLM as the thing measured, not the referee. Report all model x condition cells (as in "all fifteen signed gaps are positive") with per-episode SEs/CIs. Explicitly decline claims at small n (e.g. "n=5 models cannot support a correlation"). Avoid reading orderings from 3 models as trends.
5. **Make every number traceable and the figures complete.** Have one consolidated numbers file generated from `results/*.jsonl` that every claim cites, and a verify script that reproduces each table. Do not reference supplementary figures that are not in the PDF. Explain every n mismatch (the "task 60" problem). Include an LLM-usage statement that names Claude's roles, including as a subject if Claude models are agents. The dual-use appendix should name the specific misreading of our findings (e.g. "monitors fail -> give up on monitoring") and say why it does not follow.
